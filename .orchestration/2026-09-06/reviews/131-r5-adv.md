### Why the sentence rule does not catch it

`_is_seed_material` compares a sentence's whole word key against the seed's
sentence set and against each file's joined text. Every sentence above is
built out of seed runs that are *not adjacent in the seed*:

- `Hi Adam,` (the cold email's greeting) + `I think your background lines
  up well with what they are after.` (the tail of her second paragraph),
  joined by a comma. In the seed, `Hi Adam,` is followed by `Your name came
  up…`, so the joined key is in neither the sentence set nor any file's
  text.
- `I am filling … requisition REQ-4417 — and` (paragraph 1) +
  `the client would like to start interviews inside the next two weeks.`
  (paragraph 2), the words between them dropped.
- `My own note: ` + a whole seed sentence.

Contiguity is still the whole test; the sentence merely replaced the line
as the window it is applied over. Re-**breaking** the material (which is
what round 4's `REPASTE_SHAPES` do) now fails everywhere. Re-**joining** it
does not.

## S-1. B3's fix reintroduces the line-shape tell for a hard-wrapped candidate — a regression against `f01c1e6`

`harness/scorers/wrapping.py:78-85` (`wrap_width`), `:27` and `:33`.

`wrap_width` samples only from paragraphs of three or more lines and falls
back to the draft's **longest line** when the sample is empty or under
`MIN_WRAP_COLUMN`. A draft whose paragraphs are all one or two lines
therefore has an empty sample, the width becomes the longest line, and any
paragraph wrapped narrower than that line is no longer read as wrapped.
That is exactly the long-URL bug the docstring at `:57-63` says a median
fixes — "The column used to be the draft's longest line, so a single
unbreakable URL … became the width" — and it is back for every draft with
no three-line paragraph. `_HYPERLINKED_DRAFT` (`run_tests.py:3811-3823`)
does not catch it because its URL paragraph runs to four lines.

Measured through the production path (`judge.blind_order` →
`judge._build_pairwise_prompt`, `recruiter-reply`, trial 0), candidate = a
reply hard-wrapped at 62 with three two-line paragraphs and one long
careers URL:

```
head      draft A  10 lines, 0 surviving hard wraps   (a reference)
          draft B  10 lines, 0                        (a reference)
          draft C  12 lines, hard wraps of 63 and 66 survive   <- the agent's
f01c1e6   drafts A, B, C  10 lines each, 0 surviving wraps
```

The candidate is the only draft in the prompt that still carries hard
wrapping, on every trial, deterministically — which is the tell
`_normalize_draft_text` exists to remove and the same defect the round-4
code review rated a blocker in the mirror direction. A URL is not even
required: a draft whose paragraphs wrap at genuinely different columns
(58/72/50, 55/70/48, 50/80/45) leaves 1–2 wraps on head and none on
`f01c1e6`.

Not reproducible on any committed reference: all six, re-wrapped at every
column from 40 to 120, normalise with zero surviving wraps on both heads.

**Should-fix, not blocker,** and the reason matters: the canonical model
shape — one long line per paragraph, which `_normalize_draft_text`'s own
docstring says is what an arm hands back, and which B3 was about — is
unaffected. The shape needed here is a candidate that hard-wraps *and* has
no three-line paragraph. Round 4 rated the same class a blocker because it
fired on the default shape; this one does not.

*Fix*: when the sample is empty, fall back to the longest line **of a
paragraph that has more than one line**, or to the longest line *excluding*
lines that are longer than the next-longest by more than a word — the
docstring already names the property ("robust to one long line"), it just
stops applying it once no paragraph qualifies.
*Test*: the two-line-paragraph hyperlinked draft through
`_assert_one_line_shape` — red on head, green on `f01c1e6`.
