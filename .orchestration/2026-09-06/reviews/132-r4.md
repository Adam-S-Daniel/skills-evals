### 4(i) — does any anchor misread a `#` inside a quoted string?

**Yes.** `^[^#\n]*` cannot distinguish a comment-introducing `#` from one inside a quoted string, because it is a regex over text and shell quoting is not lexical context it can see. Two directions, measured:

- **Fail-closed (the realistic one, and a regression this round introduced):** `z6` — `git commit -m "chore: bump #${NEXT_VERSION}" --no-gpg-sign` is a *correct* fix for finding 6 and now **fails** `commit-signing-safe-for-ci`; it passed at 5b443aa, where the alternative was unanchored. Ordering decides it: `z5` puts `--no-gpg-sign` before the `#`-bearing message and passes. Commit messages carrying `#<number>` are ordinary.
- **Fail-open:** `q4` — `echo "phase #1"; set -e` evades decoy 2's bare-`set -e` ban with nothing compensating. Contrived. For `q1`/`q2`/`q3` the same `#` blocks the corresponding `must_match` too, so those checks still fail — correct verdict, reached by accident.

This is the honest price of the lexical design and I would not re-engineer the anchors for it; what is missing is that **the fixture header does not say so** (see should-fix S2 and §5).
